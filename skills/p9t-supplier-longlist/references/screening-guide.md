# Screening Guide

How to interpret must-have and nice-to-have criteria when building a supplier longlist.

---

## Must-haves vs. nice-to-haves

| Type | Definition | Consequence of failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Must-have** | Non-negotiable requirement. Absence disqualifies the candidate regardless of other strengths. | Remove from longlist. Document reason. |
| **Nice-to-have** | Preferred but not blocking. Contributes to relative ranking or confidence score. | Retain on longlist. Note the gap. |

**Rule**: Must-haves must be confirmed with the user before screening begins. Do not infer must-haves from the brief — ask explicitly.

---

## Evidence standards per criterion type

| Criterion type | Acceptable evidence | Not sufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Certification (e.g., ISO 14001) | Certificate reference, expiry date, issuing body | Supplier's own claim without reference |
| Revenue / financial threshold | Published accounts, Companies House / KvK / national register | Undated financials, supplier self-declaration |
| Geographic coverage | Named delivery locations with operational evidence | "We can cover globally" without specifics |
| Sector experience | Named references or publicly disclosed client relationships | "We work in many sectors" |
| Headcount / FTE | Verified operational headcount in the relevant service line | Total company headcount |

**Rule**: If the evidence is absent, record the criterion as "unverified" — not as met or not met. Do not disqualify on absence of evidence alone unless the criterion is a verified must-have.

---

## Handling partial evidence

When a candidate partially meets a must-have criterion:

1. Record the specific gap
2. Assess whether the gap is fatal (category-level disqualifier) or verifiable (could be confirmed with one call)
3. If verifiable: retain on longlist with a "pending verification" flag
4. If fatal: remove from longlist with documented reason

Do not present partially evidenced candidates as fully qualified.

---

## Escalation protocol

Escalate to the user before finalising the longlist when:

- Fewer than 3 candidates meet all must-have criteria → criteria may be too restrictive; review before proceeding
- A candidate meets criteria but is flagged by stakeholders for non-documented reasons → surface the flag explicitly; do not silently remove
- Must-have criteria are contradictory (e.g., two certifications that are mutually exclusive) → ask user to resolve before applying
- The only passing candidates are closely related (same parent company, joint venture) → flag concentration risk

---

## Confidence notes per candidate

Each candidate record should include a confidence note:

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **HIGH** | All must-haves verified from independent sources; evidence is current and specific |
| **MEDIUM** | Most must-haves verified; one or two items are self-declared or pending confirmation |
| **LOW** | Evidence is sparse or relies primarily on the candidate's own materials; further verification recommended before qualification |

Confidence notes inform the qualification stage — a LOW-confidence longlist entry should be prioritised for evidence gathering before scoring.
