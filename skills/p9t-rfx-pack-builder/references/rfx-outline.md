# RFx Outline

Suggested sections for RFx packs. Adapt to the event type (RFI, RFP, RFQ, ITT) and category. Not every section is required for every event — omit sections that do not apply and document the reason.

---

## Section 1 — Instructions to Bidders

Purpose: tell suppliers how to respond, not what to respond with.

Contents:
- Event overview and purpose
- Timeline: issue date, clarification deadline, submission deadline, evaluation period, award decision date
- Submission format and channel (e.g., portal, email, hard copy)
- Clarification process — how questions are submitted and how answers are shared
- Confidentiality obligations
- Right to cancel, withdraw, or re-run
- Disqualification conditions (e.g., late submission, material misrepresentation)

**Rule**: Instructions must be unambiguous. Ambiguous instructions produce incomparable responses.

## Section 2 — Background and context

Purpose: give suppliers enough context to respond intelligently.

Contents:
- Organisation overview (brief — not a marketing document)
- Category scope and current situation
- Objectives for this sourcing event
- Volume and spend envelope (indicative)
- Sites or locations in scope

## Section 3 — Scope of services / requirements

Purpose: define what the supplier is being asked to provide.

Contents:
- Service or product specification by lot (if multi-lot)
- Performance requirements and KPIs
- SLA structure (response times, resolution times, availability targets)
- Transition and mobilisation requirements
- Constraints (site access, working hours, safety requirements)

**Rule**: Requirements must be technology-neutral unless a specific standard is mandatory for a documented reason. OEM-specific language must be reviewed before issue.

## Section 4 — Commercial schedule

Purpose: define how pricing will be structured and submitted.

Contents:
- Pricing template (see `pricing-template-guidance.md`)
- Commercial model (fixed price / unit rate / open book / gain share)
- Indexation and variation mechanism
- Payment terms
- Penalties and incentives (SLA breach, performance bonus)

**Rule**: The pricing template must accommodate the commercial models expected from the market. A template that only fits one model produces incomparable responses.

## Section 5 — Evaluation criteria

Purpose: tell suppliers how their response will be assessed.

Contents:
- Evaluation dimensions and weights (see `scorecard-patterns.md`)
- Scoring methodology (criteria, scale, anchors)
- Qualification gate (pass/fail criteria that apply before weighted scoring)
- What happens in the event of a tie

**Rule**: Evaluation criteria must be disclosed to suppliers. Hidden criteria produce legally challengeable awards.

## Section 6 — Compliance and declarations

Purpose: capture mandatory compliance attestations.

Contents:
- Financial standing (accounts, credit rating, auditor letter)
- Insurance requirements (employer liability, public liability, professional indemnity)
- Legal and regulatory compliance declarations
- Conflict-of-interest declaration
- Sustainability / ESG commitment (if mandatory)
- Data protection and information security confirmation

## Section 7 — Response format

Purpose: define exactly what the supplier must submit and in what structure.

Contents:
- Response sections mapped to evaluation criteria
- Word or page limits per section
- Required appendices
- Acceptable file formats

**Rule**: If a response section does not map to an evaluation criterion, remove it. Sections that are not evaluated waste supplier effort and create reviewer inconsistency.
