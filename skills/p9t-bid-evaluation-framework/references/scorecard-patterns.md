# Scorecard Patterns

Common weighted scorecard patterns for sourcing events. Use as a starting point — adapt weights to the specific category context and stakeholder priorities.

---

## Dimension categories

| Dimension | Typical sub-criteria |
|---|---|
| **Commercial** | Total cost of ownership, price trajectory, payment terms, cost transparency |
| **Technical / capability** | Fit to specification, delivery capability, innovation, scalability |
| **Service / quality** | SLA commitment, account management, responsiveness, references |
| **Risk** | Financial stability, supply chain resilience, regulatory compliance, ESG |
| **Implementation** | Mobilisation plan, transition risk, timeline credibility |

---

## Typical weight ranges by sourcing type

| Sourcing type | Commercial | Technical | Service | Risk | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commodity / low complexity | 50–65% | 15–20% | 10–15% | 10–15% | — |
| Managed services / moderate | 30–40% | 25–30% | 20–25% | 10–15% | 5–10% |
| Strategic / complex | 25–35% | 25–30% | 20–25% | 10–15% | 5–10% |
| Safety-critical | 20–25% | 30–40% | 15–20% | 20–30% | 5–10% |

**Rule**: Weights must sum to 100. Weights must be set before bids are received.

---

## Scoring scale options

### 5-point scale (recommended for most events)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Exceeds requirement — specific, evidenced, adds value |
| 4 | Fully meets requirement — clear commitment, credible plan |
| 3 | Mostly meets requirement — minor gaps or qualifications |
| 2 | Partially meets — significant gaps or unclear commitments |
| 1 | Fails to meet — unacceptable or no response |

### 3-point scale (suitable for simple / rapid events)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | Meets or exceeds |
| 2 | Partial / qualified |
| 1 | Does not meet |

**Avoid 10-point scales** unless calibration across evaluators is tightly controlled — mid-point clustering reduces discriminating power.

---

## Anchor definition guidance

Each criterion must have written anchors for each score level. Without anchors, evaluators apply different standards, producing unreliable aggregated scores.

Example — SLA commitment:

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Named SLA targets exceed specification; penalty regime is credible; references available |
| 3 | SLA targets meet specification; penalty structure present but vague |
| 1 | SLA targets below specification or not provided |

---

## Overlap detection

A framework has overlapping criteria when the same supplier attribute can be scored in two or more dimensions. Signs of overlap:

- "Financial stability" appears in both **Risk** and **Commercial**
- "Experience" appears in both **Technical** and **Service**
- "References" appear in multiple sub-criteria

**Rule**: Each attribute should be scoreable in exactly one criterion. If overlap cannot be eliminated, the heavier-weighted criterion takes precedence and the other is removed.

---

## Pre-issue checks

Before the framework is used to score live bids:

1. Weights sum to 100 ✓
2. Each criterion has written scoring anchors ✓
3. No criterion overlaps with another ✓
4. Framework was finalised before bids were received ✓
5. All mandatory compliance or regulatory criteria are present ✓
6. Evaluation panel has been briefed and signed the conflict-of-interest declaration ✓
