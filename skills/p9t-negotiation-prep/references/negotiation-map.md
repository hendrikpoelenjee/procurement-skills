# Negotiation Map

Framework for defining positions, concessions, and leverage. Provides the structure for a negotiation plan — not a script.

---

## Core concepts

### ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement)

The range between the buyer's walk-away point and the supplier's walk-away point. A deal is possible only within this zone. If the zones do not overlap, no agreement is reachable without one party adjusting its position.

- **Buyer walk-away**: the point below which the deal is worse than the best alternative
- **Supplier walk-away**: the point below which the supplier will not proceed

The buyer rarely knows the supplier's true walk-away. The negotiation plan should include a hypothesis and a method to test it.

### BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)

The credible alternative if this negotiation fails. A strong BATNA gives the buyer real leverage. A weak or theoretical BATNA does not.

- State the BATNA explicitly in the plan
- Assess whether the supplier knows or suspects the BATNA
- Never overstate the BATNA — a bluff that is called destroys credibility

## Position structure

| Position type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening position** | The first offer made — typically ambitious but defensible | 15% reduction on current rates |
| **Target position** | The outcome the buyer genuinely aims to achieve | 10% reduction + improved SLA |
| **Walk-away point** | The minimum acceptable outcome — must be approved before negotiation begins | 5% reduction; no SLA degradation |

**Rule**: Walk-away point must be approved by the decision authority before any negotiation session begins. It must not be moved during negotiation without a new approval.

## Leverage categories

| Leverage type | Strong when | Weak when |
|---|---|---|
| **Alternatives** | 2+ qualified suppliers available | Sole source or incumbent only |
| **Timing** | Supplier needs the contract renewal now | Buyer has no alternative at contract expiry |
| **Volume** | Buyer represents significant share of supplier revenue | Buyer is a small account |
| **Information** | Buyer has market benchmark pricing | Supplier has knowledge of buyer's constraints |
| **Relationship** | Neutral or new supplier | Incumbent with deep internal relationships |

Assess each leverage type and rate it HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW. Do not plan tactics that depend on leverage the buyer does not actually have.

---

## Concession sequencing

Concessions should be planned before the negotiation, not improvised during it.

**Rules**:
1. Never concede without receiving something in return
2. Concede on lower-priority items first; protect higher-priority items
3. Make concessions smaller over time — signal that the limit is approaching
4. Do not volunteer concessions; let the supplier ask for them

| Concession | Value to supplier | Cost to buyer | When to offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extended payment terms | Medium | Low | Early — low cost to buyer |
| Longer contract duration | High | Medium | Mid — useful if price is stuck |
| Exclusivity for category | High | High | Late and only if necessary |
| Reduced SLA penalty regime | Medium | Medium | Only if supplier raises it |

## MESOs (Multiple Equivalent Simultaneous Offers)

Present two or three package options to the supplier that are equivalent in value to the buyer, but differ in structure. This reveals supplier preferences and creates room to move without simply discounting.

Example:
- Option A: price reduction of 10%, 2-year term
- Option B: price reduction of 7%, 3-year term with volume commitment
- Option C: price reduction of 5%, 3-year term with extended scope

---

## Leverage inversion risks

Flags that indicate the buyer's leverage is weaker than assumed:

- Supplier has knowledge of the buyer's internal constraints (budget ceiling, timeline pressure)
- Contract expiry is closer than the time required to qualify an alternative supplier
- Incumbent has proprietary systems, data, or relationships that create practical lock-in
- The buyer's BATNA has not been formally validated with the alternative supplier

Flag these explicitly in the negotiation plan. Do not build a plan around leverage that may not exist.

---

## Plan confidentiality

Walk-away positions and concession plans are commercially sensitive. They must:
- Not be shared with the supplier, directly or indirectly
- Not be accessible to any party with a relationship conflict with the supplier
- Be treated as restricted internally — access limited to the negotiation team and approver
